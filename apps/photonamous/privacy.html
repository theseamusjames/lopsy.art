Privacy Policy

Effective November 24, 2026

Photonamous is a photo library and RAW editor that runs in your web browser, at photonamous.com. It is run by Seamus James (“we”).

The short version: Photonamous has no accounts, and we run no server that receives your photos. Your photos, your edits and your library are kept in your own Google Drive and in your browser. We never see them, and we never learn your name or your email address. Two services work on our behalf: Google Analytics, which gives us usage statistics that do not say who you are, and Cloudflare, the web host, which handles the requests your browser makes for the site.

What we collect, and what we don’t

Your name and email address: not collected

There is no Photonamous account. When you sign in with Google, you give the app permission to use Google Drive and nothing else. The app does not ask Google for your name, email address or profile picture, and Google does not send them.

Your photos, edits and library: never sent to us

They are kept in two places, both yours:

  • Your Google Drive. The app creates a folder named “Photonamous” there and keeps in it your original photos, documents saved from Lopsy, your exports, and the library’s own data: its record of your edits, ratings, keywords and settings, preview images, and a short note of each device you have used (such as “Chrome on macOS”, the app’s version and when it was last used).
  • This browser, on your device. The app keeps working copies in your browser’s storage so that it is quick and works offline: a copy of the library’s data, previews, photos you have opened or are importing, changes waiting to be saved, and a few settings. If your browser’s WebGPU graphics fail to start, the tab notes that until it is closed, so the editor uses WebGL instead.

These travel directly between your browser and Google. They do not pass through, and are not stored on, any server of ours. We cannot see them, and we cannot recover or delete them for you.

How Photonamous uses your Google data

Photonamous asks Google for one permission, which Google describes as “See, edit, create and delete only the specific Google Drive files you use with this app” (the drive.file scope).

What it can access

  • The files and folders the app itself creates in your Drive: the “Photonamous” folder and what is in it.
  • Files you choose for it yourself with Google’s file picker, when you use Add from Google Drive. The app can then read those files, wherever in your Drive they are.
  • How much Drive storage you have used and have left, so the app can show you.
  • Drive’s ID number for your Google account, described below.

It cannot see or open anything else in your Drive, and it has no access to the rest of your Google account.

What it does with it

  • It uses these files only to do what you see it do: store, show, organise, edit and export your photos, and keep your library the same on each device you sign in from.
  • A photo you add with the picker is read, added to your library and labelled as part of it. Depending on the choice you make in Settings, it is moved into the “Photonamous” folder or left where it is.
  • When you delete a photo in the app, its file is moved to Google Drive’s trash. The only files the app deletes outright are its own out-of-date copies of the library’s data.

Where it is stored

In your Google Drive and in this browser, as described above, and nowhere else. The access token Google issues is held only in the page’s memory. Each one expires after about an hour; while you are using the app it asks Google for a new one before the old one runs out, and it asks Google to revoke the token when you close your library.

Who it is shared with

No one. We do not receive your Google data, so we cannot share it, sell it, use it for advertising, let anyone read it, or use it to develop or train artificial intelligence or machine learning models. Two things happen only when you ask for them:

  • Copy share link on an export asks Google Drive to make that one export’s folder viewable by anyone who has the link. You can stop sharing it in the app or in Google Drive.
  • Edit in Lopsy hands a photo to Lopsy inside your browser, described below.

Photonamous’ use of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

The account ID kept in your browser

So that two Google accounts used in the same browser never mix, the app asks Google Drive for its ID for your account. It is a number that says nothing about who you are. The app keeps it only in this browser’s storage, uses it to keep each account’s working copies apart and to reconnect to the right account, and never sends it to us or to anyone else.

Usage statistics (Google Analytics)

We use Google Analytics, loaded through Google Tag Manager, to count visits and to learn which parts of Photonamous are used. It is the only analytics on the site.

  • It sets cookies in your browser to tell one visitor from another, and may record the pages and screens you open, actions such as pressing a button, your browser, device type and language, the site you came from, and your approximate location (country and city). Google Analytics works that location out from your IP address and does not log or store the address itself.
  • We do not send it your photos, file names, anything from your Google Drive, or your account ID. We do not use it for advertising.
  • Google Analytics keeps visit-level records for up to 14 months. Totals that identify no one may be kept longer.

To opt out, install the Google Analytics Opt-out Browser Add-on, or block cookies for photonamous.com; Photonamous works the same without it. Google explains how it uses information from sites that use its services.

Our web host (Cloudflare)

Photonamous is a set of static files served by Cloudflare. Like any web host, Cloudflare handles each request your browser makes for those files, which includes your IP address, your browser’s description of itself, the file requested and the time, in order to deliver the site and protect it from abuse. Cloudflare may show us totals, such as how many requests the site received. We run no server, database or log of our own. Cloudflare’s privacy policy describes what it does with this information.

Other services

Google

Signing in, the file picker and Google Drive are Google’s services. Your use of them is covered by Google’s Privacy Policy, and Google knows that you use Photonamous with your account, as it does for any app you connect.

Lopsy

Edit in Lopsy opens Lopsy, a separate image editor at lopsy.art, in another tab and hands the photo to it directly, inside your browser. The photo is not uploaded to a server on the way. When you save back, Lopsy hands the result to Photonamous the same way, and Photonamous saves it to your Google Drive. Lopsy is a separate site, and opening it is a visit to that site.

Security

Everything the app sends travels between your browser and Google over an encrypted connection (HTTPS). In your Google Drive, your files are protected by Google and by your Google account. The copies in your browser are as private as your device and browser profile are: someone who can use your browser profile can open them.

Keeping and deleting your data

We hold none of your photos or library data, so there is nothing for us to keep or delete. You can remove everything yourself:

  • Sign out. Close this library in the app signs you out and revokes the app’s access token.
  • Remove the app’s permission. In your Google Account, open Third-party apps and services and remove Photonamous.
  • Delete the files. Delete the “Photonamous” folder in Google Drive. Photos you added from elsewhere in your Drive and chose to leave in place stay where they are.
  • Clear this browser’s copies. Clear the site data for photonamous.com in your browser’s settings, on each device and browser you have used.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, the law may give you rights over information about you, such as to see it, correct it or have it deleted. The only information we can see is the usage statistics described above, which do not tell us who you are. If you have a question or a request, contact us and we will help as far as we can. For what Google holds, use the controls in your Google Account.

Children

Photonamous is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them.

Changes to this policy

If the policy changes, we will publish the new version on this page and change the date at the top. We have no email address for you, so we cannot write to tell you; please look here from time to time. Earlier versions can be seen in the history of the public source repository.

Contact
